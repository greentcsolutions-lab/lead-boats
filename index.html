The quiet risks

The three things killing industrial suppliers slowly

01 — Concentration risk

Your revenue sits in a handful of accounts

Three, four, maybe five customers carry the payroll. You know what happens if one of them changes a specification, gets acquired, or finds a cheaper source. You don't have a growth problem — you have a risk problem nobody says out loud in the management meeting.

02 — Unpredictable pipeline

The pipeline looks like a heartbeat

A strong quarter, then silence. Bookings come from referrals and the same relationships your reps have had for ten years, so you can't forecast anything beyond 60 days. Capacity sits idle in the quiet months and gets hammered in the good ones.

03 — Commodity trap

Buyers treat your product like a commodity

They send the same RFQ to you and three competitors, then buy on price and lead time. The buyer never sees what makes your product better because a spec sheet and a PDF never showed anyone anything.

The mechanism

We do one thing

Most agencies sell you marketing: ads, a website, a monthly report, a retainer, then a list of "leads" your team has to chase for three weeks. That's not the part of the business that hurts.

We find the companies in your target market worth having, reach the people who actually specify and approve industrial products — engineering, maintenance, procurement, operations — and get them to request a sample kit.

A meeting is easy to ignore. A sample request isn't. A buyer who asks for a sample has already decided your product is worth evaluating.

The sample lands on their desk, your product gets handled, and your team walks into a conversation that's already half-won — instead of cold-calling a stranger who's never heard of you.

How it works

1

We take your specifications, your margins and your ideal buyer, then build a target account list of the companies and the decision-makers inside them. Regions your reps never get to. Accounts held by competitors. Buyers who fit your product but have never heard your name.

2

We reach the people who specify and approve

Outreach goes to the person who decides whether your product gets trialled — not the generic info@ inbox. We write the approach in industrial language, not marketing language. No hype, no stock photos of handshakes.

3

Buyers request sample kits. We route them to you.

Every request lands with your team, with the buyer's details and what they asked for. You ship the sample and your salespeople do what they're good at: closing. Fulfilment, sample cost and shipping stay with you — we generate the demand.

Questions

Common questions before booking

What exactly counts as a sample kit request?

A request from a decision-maker or influencer at a company that meets the target market criteria we agree with you in writing before we start — job titles, company size, geography, and product fit. We lock the definition with you before any outreach begins.

Who pays for the samples and the shipping?

You do. We generate the request; you fulfil it. That's the only part of this we can't do for you — and it's the part that makes the request worth so much, because a buyer holding your product is a real buyer.

What happens if you don't hit 30 requests?

You don't pay. If we haven't generated 30 qualified sample kit requests within 90 days of the target list and outreach copy being approved, you owe us nothing — no invoice, no fee. The only conditions: you approve the target list and copy, and you ship samples within a reasonable turnaround of each request.

How long does it take?

90 days from the day the target list and outreach copy are approved. Approvals are the one thing that can slow this down.

My market is narrow. Can you still hit 30?

Possibly, and we'll tell you on the call if we don't think we can. A tightly defined market of a few hundred accounts is often easier than a broad one — the buyers are simply harder to find, which is the work we do. If it's too small for 30 requests, we'll say so rather than take the fee.

Do we need a CRM, a marketing team or new software?

No. We need a contact for sample routing, someone to approve copy and the target list, and a way to tell us which requests turned into quotes.

Does this replace our salespeople?

No — and it shouldn't. Your reps keep the relationships and close the business. We create the conversations they currently don't have time to start.

What does it cost?

We'll go through it on the call once we've seen your market. Pricing is based on a pay-per-lead model — we only bill on the sample kit requests generated.
